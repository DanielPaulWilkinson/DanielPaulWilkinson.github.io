Unslop

Detect and remove AI writing patterns. Scan text for puffery, vague attributions, AI vocabulary, and style tells. Rewrite for clarity and human voice.

Remove AI patterns and add human voice to text, documentation, comments, and generated content.

Process

  1. Scan for patterns below.
  2. Rewrite — preserve meaning, match intended tone.
  3. Add soul (see next section).
  4. Self-audit: "What makes this obviously AI generated?" Fix remaining tells.

Adding soul

Removing patterns is half the job. Sterile, voiceless writing is just as obvious.

  • Have opinions. React to facts instead of neutrally listing pros and cons.
  • Vary rhythm. Short sentences. Then longer ones that take their time. Mix it up.
  • Acknowledge complexity. "Impressive but also kind of unsettling" beats "impressive."
  • Use "I" when it fits. First person isn't unprofessional.
  • Let some mess in. Perfect structure looks machine-made.
  • Be specific. Not "this is concerning" but "there's something unsettling about agents churning away at 3am."

Patterns to detect and fix

Content

  1. Puffery — "pivotal moment", "testament to", "evolving landscape", "setting the stage for", "indelible mark", "deeply rooted". Cut puffery, state what happened. - ❌ This represents a pivotal moment in the project's evolution. - ✅ This changed how the project works.

  2. Name-dropping — Listing media outlets without context. Pick one, say what was said. - ❌ According to Forbes, Bloomberg, and The New York Times, the trend is growing. - ✅ Forbes reported a 40% increase in adoption.

  3. Superficial -ing phrases — "highlighting...", "ensuring...", "reflecting...", "showcasing...", "fostering...". Delete or expand with real sources. - ❌ The update highlights the importance of security while ensuring backwards compatibility. - ✅ The update maintains backwards compatibility without breaking existing code.

  4. Promotional language — "nestled", "vibrant", "breathtaking", "groundbreaking", "renowned", "stunning", "must-visit". Use neutral descriptions. - ❌ Our groundbreaking, vibrant platform nestled in the cloud. - ✅ Our cloud-based platform.

  5. Vague attributions — "Experts believe", "Industry reports suggest", "Some critics argue". Name the source or delete. - ❌ Experts suggest this approach improves performance. - ✅ Benchmarks show a 25% performance improvement.

  6. Formulaic challenges — "Despite challenges... continues to thrive." Replace with specific facts. - ❌ Despite challenges, the team continues to innovate. - ✅ The team shipped three features despite losing two developers to other projects.

Language

  1. AI vocabulary — Additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape (abstract), pivotal, showcase, tapestry (abstract), testament, underscore, vibrant. Replace with plain words. - ❌ We delve into the intricate interplay of components to enhance performance. - ✅ We examine how components interact to improve performance.

  2. Fancy ways to say "is" — "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features". Just say "is" or "has". - ❌ This tool serves as a powerful solution that boasts enterprise features. - ✅ This tool is powerful and has enterprise features.

  3. "Not just X, but Y" — State the point directly instead. - ❌ This is not just a library, but a complete ecosystem. - ✅ This is a complete ecosystem.

  4. Rule of three — Forcing ideas into groups of three. Use the natural number. - ❌ The system is fast, reliable, and scalable. - ✅ The system is fast and reliable. (Keep only what matters.)

  5. Synonym cycling — protagonist, main character, central figure, hero all in one paragraph. Pick one, repeat it. - ❌ The protagonist faces the antagonist. Our main character must overcome the villain. The hero emerges victorious. - ✅ The protagonist faces the antagonist and emerges victorious.

  6. False ranges — "from X to Y" where X and Y aren't on a meaningful scale. List topics directly. - ❌ Topics range from setup to advanced configuration. - ✅ Setup, basic configuration, and advanced tuning.

Style

  1. Em dash overuse — Avoid em dashes entirely. Use periods or commas only (no parentheses, no en dashes, no hyphen-as-dash substitutes). Em dashes are an AI tell. - ❌ The API handles requests — including edge cases — with grace. - ✅ The API handles requests, including edge cases, with grace.

  2. Colon overuse — Colons are fine before a list or example. Not as mid-sentence connectors. - ❌ If you're coming from automation: instead of registering handlers, describe conditions. - ✅ Describing conditions works better than registering handlers.

  3. Boldface overuse — Don't bold every proper noun or acronym. - ❌ TypeScript and React are JavaScript tools. - ✅ TypeScript and React are JavaScript tools.

  4. Inline-header lists — The tell is a bold label and colon that restates the line: "Performance: Performance improved...". Convert to prose. (A bold lead-in that ends in a period and is followed by new detail is fine: "Schema in TypeScript. Tables live in one file.") - ❌ Benefits: Our approach provides better performance, easier maintenance, and faster development. - ✅ Our approach performs better, is easier to maintain, and speeds up development.

  5. Title case headings — Use sentence case. - ❌ # Important Configuration Details - ✅ # Important configuration details

  6. Decorative emojis — Remove from headings and bullets. - ❌ ## 🚀 Getting Started - ✅ ## Getting started

  7. Curly quotes — Replace with straight quotes. - ❌ "Use straight quotes," not "curly ones." - ✅ "Use straight quotes," not "curly ones."

Communication artifacts

  1. Chatbot phrases — "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if...", "Of course!", "Certainly!", "Found the smoking gun!" Remove. - ❌ I hope this helps! Let me know if you have questions. - ✅ (End naturally without filler.)

  2. Cutoff disclaimers — "While specific details are limited..." Find sources or remove. - ❌ While specific details are limited, reports suggest growth. - ✅ [Provide specific data or remove the claim.]

  3. Sycophantic tone — "Great question! You're absolutely right!" Respond directly. - ❌ Great question! You're absolutely right about the performance impact. - ✅ The performance impact occurs because...

Filler

  1. Filler phrases — "In order to" becomes "To". "Due to the fact that" becomes "Because". "It is important to note that" gets deleted. - ❌ In order to understand this, it is important to note that, due to the fact that the system is distributed, responses may vary. - ✅ Because the system is distributed, responses may vary.

  2. Excessive hedging — "could potentially possibly be argued that it might" becomes "may". - ❌ It could potentially be argued that this might improve performance. - ✅ This may improve performance.

  3. Generic conclusions — "The future looks bright." State specific plans or facts. - ❌ The future looks bright for this technology. - ✅ Version 2.0 ships in Q4 with streaming support.

Jargon

  1. Abstract metaphor nouns — substrate, wedge, vector, locus, vantage, nexus, primitive (as noun), harness (as metaphor), surface (as in "API surface"), bedrock, scaffolding (as metaphor), modality, paradigm, gold-plating, ratchet (as metaphor), evacuate (for moving code), endgame, north star, flywheel. These read as technical but usually have a plainer concrete word. - ❌ We're leveraging this as a wedge into the enterprise market, using it as a north star for future development. - ✅ We're using this to enter the enterprise market and guiding future development by it.

Examples of replacements:

  • "Substrate" → "base"
  • "Wedge in" → "add"
  • "Vector" → "way" or "method"
  • "Gold-plating" → "more than the job needs"
  • "Ratchet" → the mechanism's real name or "a limit that only tightens"
  • "Evacuate" → "move out"
  • "Endgame" → "the last phase"
  • "North star" → "goal" or "target"

Plain speech

  1. Say what it does, not how it feels — "the database stays close at hand", "SQL you can read", "types that follow your schema" name a feeling. The fix names the mechanism or a number. - ❌ This library brings types close at hand, letting you write SQL you can read. - ✅ .toSQL() returns the exact string sent to the database. Type definitions auto-sync with schema.

Ask: what should the reader do or know? If you can't restate it as a concrete instruction, fact, or number, cut it.

  1. Shorten or split dense sentences — If the reader has to backtrack to parse a sentence, break it in two or drop clauses. - ❌ The middleware, which processes requests before they reach handlers, validates input against a schema defined in the config, ensuring type safety across the entire stack. - ✅ The middleware validates input against the schema. This ensures type safety. Handlers receive clean data.

  2. Active voice — Prefer it. Catch "is/are/was/were + past participle" and name the actor. - ❌ Queries are validated by the compiler. The file is parsed by the loader. - ✅ The compiler validates queries. The loader parses the file.

Passive is fine only when the actor is unknown or genuinely doesn't matter.

  1. Cut adverbs, or use a stronger verb — "runs quickly" becomes "is fast" or the number. "significantly improves" becomes the measured delta. - ❌ The update significantly improves performance. - ✅ The update adds a 40% performance boost.

  2. Prefer the plain word — utilize → use, leverage → use, facilitate → help, numerous → many, in the event that → if. - ❌ We facilitate numerous improvements by utilizing this approach. - ✅ We improve many things using this approach.

Workflow

For any piece of text:

  1. Read the text with fresh eyes.
  2. Mark patterns as you spot them — write the line number or quote.
  3. Rewrite each pattern using the fixes above. Preserve the original meaning.
  4. Read aloud — does it sound human? Natural rhythm?
  5. Self-audit: Could this appear unchanged in a different project's docs? Cut it. Does every sentence do work? Keep only what matters.
  6. Final check: No puffery, no vague attribution, no AI vocabulary, no false ranges, no filler. ✅

When to use this skill

  • Code review of documentation, comments, or generated text
  • Writing blog posts, READMEs, or technical guides
  • Reviewing AI-generated content before publishing
  • Editing your own writing to strip robotic phrasing
  • Training models or tuning LLM outputs
