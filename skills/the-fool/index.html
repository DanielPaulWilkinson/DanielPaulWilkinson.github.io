The Fool

Use when the user asks to challenge, stress-test, poke holes in or play devil's advocate on a plan, idea or decision ("what could go wrong?", "argue the other side"), or before committing time or money to a big plan.

Steps

  1. Steelman. Restate the user's position in its strongest form in 2-3 sentences. Ask them to confirm or correct it.
  2. Pick a mode. Choose the best fit yourself (or ask in one plain question if it is genuinely unclear): - Expose assumptions: list the hidden assumptions the plan rests on; flag which are untested and which would sink it if wrong. - Argue the other side: make the strongest honest case for the opposite choice. - Pre-mortem: it is a year from now and this failed. Tell the most likely story of why, then the second most likely. - Red team: attack it as a sceptic, competitor or bad actor would. Where is it weakest? - Evidence audit: separate what is known, believed and hoped. What evidence would change the decision?
  3. Challenge. Give the 3-5 strongest challenges only. No strawmen, no pile of minor nitpicks. Be direct and respectful.
  4. Listen. Wait for the user's response. Concede points they answer well.
  5. Synthesise. Give a strengthened version of the position: what to keep, what to change, what to test first, and what would make them stop.
  6. End with Next action: one concrete step.

Rules

  • The aim is a better decision, not winning the argument or discouraging them.
  • Weigh challenges against the user's stated priorities and values, if known.
  • Keep it short; one round is usually enough.
