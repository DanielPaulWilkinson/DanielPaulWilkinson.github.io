Weekly admin triage

Use once a week, or when someone says their admin is piling up, to sort every open admin item into do now, schedule, delegate or ignore. Written for people juggling family, work and church or community commitments. Keep it to about 20 minutes.

A short weekly routine that turns a messy pile of admin into four short lists. The goal is a clear head and a realistic week, not an empty inbox.

When to use it

  • A set weekly slot (for example Sunday evening or Monday morning).
  • Any time the person says "I've got loads of admin", "I keep forgetting things" or "where do I start?".
  • Not for one urgent task: just help with that task.

Before you start

Ask the person for:

  1. The pile. Everything on their mind: emails needing a reply, forms, bills, bookings, school or club messages, church or volunteer rota requests, things people asked them to do. A rough brain-dump list is fine.
  2. Their week. The fixed commitments they already have (work hours, family time, regular meetings) and roughly how much admin time they have free.
  3. Who can help. Anyone they share admin with: a partner, a colleague, a volunteer team, a church office or club secretary.

If you can read their calendar, email or task list and they have said you may, use it. Otherwise work only from what they tell you. Never send, pay, book or reply to anything without their explicit yes.

Sort into four lists

Go through the pile one item at a time. For each, decide:

List Put it here when What happens next
Do now It takes under about 10 minutes, or it is due within 2 days, or something breaks if it slips Do it in this session or today
Schedule It matters but needs a longer block or has a later deadline Give it a specific day and time slot this week or next
Delegate Someone else is better placed, already responsible, or it is shared Name the person and draft the one-line ask
Ignore No one will notice if it is not done, it is someone else's job, or it is "nice to have" Drop it, archive it, or say no politely

Rules of thumb:

  • If you are unsure between Do now and Schedule, choose Schedule with a real date.
  • If an item has sat on the list for 3 weeks, it is probably Ignore or Delegate. Say so.
  • Protect family time and rest. Do not schedule admin into time the person has already set aside for family, worship or sleep unless they ask.
  • Say no to new volunteer or rota requests only if the person wants to; offer a short, kind wording.

Output

Give back one short message:

  1. Do now (max 5): each with the first physical step ("Open the school email and tick the trip form").
  2. Schedule: each with a suggested day and time slot, and how long it needs.
  3. Delegate: each with who and a one-line draft message for the person to send themselves.
  4. Ignore: a short list, so they can feel the relief of dropping it.
  5. Next action: the single thing to do in the next 10 minutes.

Keep the whole reply readable on a phone in under a minute.

Weekly review (2 minutes)

At the start of next week's triage, ask: what from last week's Schedule list did not happen? Move each item to Do now, re-schedule it once, or drop it. Notice patterns (for example, the same kind of form every month) and suggest one small system to stop it recurring, such as a standing reminder or a shared family calendar.

Rules

  • Simple beats complete. Four lists, one message, about 20 minutes.
  • Use plain language, no productivity jargon.
  • Never take an action on the person's behalf (sending, paying, booking, signing up) without their explicit yes for that specific action.
  • Do not store or repeat passwords, bank details or other credentials; if an item needs one, the person handles that step themselves.
